A congestion‑counting gap in Lemma 4.2

On “A tight meta-theorem for LOCAL certification of MSO2 properties within bounded treewidth graphs”

Linda Cook, Eun Jung Kim, Tomáš Masařík — arXiv:2503.19671

Posted — reviewed with one of the authors prior to publication

Lemma 4.2 builds an oriented path system that witnesses an elimination tree, using a k-colorable guidance system to bound how many paths can pile up on any single vertex. The congestion argument in that proof has a gap: it silently assumes something about path endpoints that the construction doesn't actually guarantee.

The setup

For each non-root vertex v of the elimination tree F whose parent p(v) isn't adjacent to it in G, the proof manufactures a path Pv simulating that missing edge. Both v and p(v) sit in the adhesion of some tree node t, and the guidance system supplies a single vertex x that "captures" that adhesion — meaning both v and p(v) are roots of in-trees containing x. The path is built by concatenating two pieces:

  • x back to v, along the in-tree of Λ rooted at v (reversed);
  • x onward to p(v), along the in-tree of Λ rooted at p(v).

Two different in-trees, glued at x.

The claimed bound

To bound congestion at an arbitrary internal vertex y, the proof collects the set S = {s1,…,sℓ} of roots of in-trees passing through y. Because Λ is k-colorable, any set of pairwise-intersecting in-trees has size at most k, so ℓ ≤ k. The proof then states:

flagged step
“…we add a new oriented path to P connecting one vertex in {s1,…,sℓ} to another vertex in {s1,…,sℓ} only when the latter vertex is the parent of the former vertex in F.” Proof of Lemma 4.2, p. 15

From this, every path through y is treated as an edge of a forest on S, giving at most ℓ − 1 ≤ k − 1 such paths.

Where it breaks

The forest bound needs both endpoints of every path through y to land in S. But Pv is stitched from two separate in-trees at x, and y only has to lie on one of them for Pv to count. If y sits on the x → p(v) leg only, that forces p(v) ∈ S — but nothing forces v ∈ S, since the in-tree rooted at v need never come near y. Symmetrically for the other leg. Only when y = x exactly are both roots pinned down at once.

So the paths crossing y don't correspond to a set of F-edges living inside S — they correspond to an arbitrary collection of half-edges hanging off S, and nothing in the colorability argument caps how many of those there can be.

A concrete overload

Take a vertex u with many F-children v1, …, vm, all routed through the same fixed in-tree rooted at u — nothing in the construction forces a different in-tree per child, since Λ need not have a unique in-tree per root. Let y be a vertex on the shared trunk of that in-tree, below u but above every capturing vertex xi. Then y is a genuine internal vertex of every Pvi, giving congestion Θ(m) at y — even though only one in-tree is involved, so k-colorability never flags a conflict. Since F can have unbounded degree, m is not controlled by k, and the claimed k − 1 bound fails.

Scope of the gap

The issue is confined to the counting step in Lemma 4.2's proof, not the higher-level argument of the paper. Lemma 4.3 — used for the actual composition over general tree-decompositions — tracks congestion more carefully, distinguishing internal-vertex occurrences from the replacement paths substituted in from Λ, and its bookkeeping doesn't appear to make the same conflation. It's plausible Lemma 4.2 is a simplified, under-specified writeup of the same idea and that the gap is a presentation issue rather than a fatal one — but as written, the stated bound of k (feeding into 3k1·k22 downstream) is not established by this proof.

What this does and doesn't affect

This note only concerns the internal congestion-counting argument used to prove Proposition 3.7's constants. It says nothing about whether the paper's main results — Theorem 1.1 and its corollaries — are false; a corrected accounting (e.g. bounding branching within each in-tree, or charging congestion per in-tree-vertex rather than per covering root) may well recover a bound polynomial in k, just not the specific one currently stated.

Status. Raised with one of the authors, who acknowledged the gap in the write-up of Lemma 4.2. This note records it for anyone else reading the paper closely; check back or contact the authors for the current state of the writeup.
