A congestion‑counting gap in Lemma 4.2

On “A tight meta-theorem for LOCAL certification of MSO2 properties within bounded treewidth graphs”

Linda Cook, Eun Jung Kim, Tomáš Masařík — arXiv:2503.19671

Posted — reviewed with one of the authors prior to publication

Lemma 4.2 builds a set of one-way paths that stand in for an elimination tree. It uses a k-colorable guidance system to limit how many paths can pass through any one vertex. But the step that counts those paths has a gap: it quietly assumes something about the paths' end points that the construction does not actually guarantee.

The setup

Take a vertex v in the elimination tree F (not the root), whose parent p(v) is not actually next to it in G. The proof builds a path Pv to stand in for that missing connection. Both v and p(v) live inside the same "adhesion" at some tree node t, and the guidance system gives us one vertex x that "reaches" both of them — meaning v and p(v) are each the root of an in-tree that contains x. The path Pv is built from two pieces glued together at x:

  • from x back to v, along the in-tree rooted at v (reversed);
  • from x onward to p(v), along the in-tree rooted at p(v).

So each path is really two different in-trees, stuck together at x.

The claimed bound

To show that not too many paths pass through some vertex y, the proof looks at the set S = {s1,…,sℓ} of all in-tree roots whose in-tree passes through y. Since Λ is k-colorable, any group of in-trees that all overlap each other has at most k members, so ℓ ≤ k. The proof then says:

flagged step
“…we add a new oriented path to P connecting one vertex in {s1,…,sℓ} to another vertex in {s1,…,sℓ} only when the latter vertex is the parent of the former vertex in F.” Proof of Lemma 4.2, p. 15

The idea is: every path through y connects two members of S, like an edge in a tree drawn on S. A tree on ℓ points has at most ℓ − 1 edges, so the proof concludes there are at most k − 1 such paths.

Where it breaks

That "edges of a tree on S" picture only works if both ends of every path through y are actually in S. But remember, Pv is glued from two separate in-trees at x. For y to lie on Pv, it only has to sit on one of those two pieces — not both. Say y sits on the piece going from x to p(v). That puts p(v) in S. But nothing says v is in S too — the in-tree rooted at v might never even pass near y. The same problem happens the other way round. The only time both roots are guaranteed to be pinned down together is the special case y = x.

So the paths going through y don't line up neatly with pairs of points in S — they're more like loose ends hanging off of S, and nothing in the coloring argument stops there from being a lot of them.

A concrete overload

Here's a case where it actually breaks. Suppose vertex u has many children in F, call them v1, …, vm, and all of them happen to route through the same in-tree rooted at u (nothing in the construction rules this out — Λ doesn't have to give a different in-tree for each root). Pick a vertex y sitting on the shared trunk of that in-tree, somewhere below u but above every "reaching" vertex xi. Now y lies inside every single path Pvi — so as many as m paths pass through it. But this only involves one in-tree, so k-colorability sees no conflict at all and never limits m. Since a vertex of F can have any number of children, m can be much bigger than k — breaking the claimed bound of k − 1.

Scope of the gap

This gap does not stay inside Lemma 4.2. Here is why. Lemma 4.3 has its own way of counting, and that counting looks fine on its own. But Lemma 4.3 does not start from scratch — it needs an input: for each marginal graph G⋆t, it needs an elimination tree and a path system with congestion at most some number k1. In the proof of Proposition 3.7, that input is built by calling Lemma 4.2. So Lemma 4.3's result depends on Lemma 4.2's congestion bound being correct.

But Lemma 4.2's congestion bound is exactly what is broken. So the number k1 that gets fed into Lemma 4.3 is not actually proven. That means Lemma 4.3's own output, 3k1·k22, is not proven either — even though the steps inside Lemma 4.3 are fine. And this broken number is exactly the bound f(ω) that Proposition 3.7 hands off, which then gets used directly in the proof of Theorem 1.1. So the gap does not just sit inside one lemma's writeup — it runs all the way from Lemma 4.2, through Lemma 4.3, through Proposition 3.7, and into the proof of the paper's main theorem.

What this does and doesn't affect

Because Lemma 4.2 feeds directly into Lemma 4.3 (through Proposition 3.7), this gap is not just a small issue in one lemma. As things are written, it reaches all the way into the proof of Theorem 1.1. That said, this does not mean Theorem 1.1 or its corollaries are false. It's likely that a fixed version of the counting argument (for example, limiting branching inside each in-tree, or counting congestion per in-tree vertex instead of per root) would still give some bound in terms of k, which could then be carried through Lemma 4.3, Proposition 3.7, and Theorem 1.1 again. But right now, as written, the proof of the main theorem has a real gap in it — not just a small, contained slip in one auxiliary lemma.

Status. Raised with one of the authors, who acknowledged the gap in the write-up of Lemma 4.2. This note records it for anyone else reading the paper closely; check back or contact the authors for the current state of the writeup.
